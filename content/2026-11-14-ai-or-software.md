# Does My Business Need AI? A Simple Test to Decide

AI is everywhere right now: in the news, in every software pitch, and in government plans. Rwanda has set up a national AI agency, and organisations across the region are being encouraged to adopt AI.

So it's natural to wonder: should we be using AI too?

Our honest answer, as a studio that builds AI systems: **sometimes, but less often than you'd think.** Many problems described as "AI projects" are better solved with ordinary software or simple automation, which costs less, breaks less often and is easier to maintain.

This guide gives you a simple test to decide, and explains what AI accuracy numbers really mean.

## Three kinds of solution

When a business wants to improve how it works, the answer usually falls into one of three buckets:

1. **Ordinary software:** a system that stores information and follows clear rules. Examples: a booking system, an order tracker, a customer database.
2. **Automation and integration:** connecting the tools you already use so information moves by itself. Example: a WhatsApp order that appears automatically in your spreadsheet and creates an invoice.
3. **AI:** systems that handle messy input or find patterns: reading documents, understanding free text, recognising objects in images, predicting outcomes.

Each is the right answer for different problems.

## The test: five questions

### 1. Could you write the rule down?

If you can describe the logic on paper ("if the order is over $500, a manager must approve it"), you don't need AI. Ordinary software will follow that rule perfectly, every time.

### 2. Does the information just need to move?

If the data already exists in one place and needs to appear in another, that's an integration. Connecting systems is usually faster and cheaper than any AI project.

### 3. Is the input messy?

This is where AI starts to make sense. Free-text customer messages, photos, scanned or handwritten documents, audio: things that don't fit neatly into a form. If people currently spend hours reading and sorting this kind of input, AI may help.

### 4. Are you looking for patterns no simple rule captures?

Predicting which customers might leave, spotting unusual transactions or estimating demand. If the pattern is too complex to write as rules, and you have enough historical data, AI or machine learning may be the right tool.

### 5. Can someone check the output?

AI is right most of the time, not all of the time. If a mistake would be costly and nobody can review the results, AI is risky. If a person can check and correct its work, at least at first, AI becomes much safer and more useful.

**How to read your answers:** if questions 1 or 2 describe your problem, start with software or automation. If questions 3 or 4 describe it and question 5 is a yes, AI is worth exploring.

## What AI accuracy numbers really mean

When someone tells you an AI system is "96% accurate", it's worth asking what that means.

We built an independent prototype called **Crosswalk Intelligence**, which uses computer vision (YOLO for detection and ByteTrack for tracking) to turn traffic footage into reviewable crossing events. We measured it on 45 events that we reviewed by hand, at one intersection in Bangkok:

- **96% precision:** when the system said "a crossing happened here", it was almost always right.
- **81% recall:** of all the real crossings, it caught about four in five.

That's a good result for a prototype. Two things stand out:

1. **It still misses some.** Every AI system has a miss rate. The question is whether your process can handle it.
2. **The context matters.** 45 events at one intersection isn't a guarantee of how it would perform elsewhere. Any honest AI result should come with that kind of caveat.

That's why we built Crosswalk to produce *reviewable* events: a person can check what the system flagged instead of trusting it blindly. We'd recommend the same principle for any business using AI.

## Common situations, and what we'd usually recommend

| Situation | Usually the right answer |
|---|---|
| "We track orders in spreadsheets and lose track of them" | Ordinary software (an order system) |
| "We copy WhatsApp orders into our accounting tool" | Automation / integration |
| "We need managers to approve big expenses" | Software with an approval workflow |
| "We receive hundreds of scanned forms and type them out by hand" | AI (document processing), with human review |
| "We want to sort customer messages by topic automatically" | AI (text classification), possibly with a simple fallback |
| "We want to predict which stock will run out" | Start with good reporting; add prediction once there's enough data |
