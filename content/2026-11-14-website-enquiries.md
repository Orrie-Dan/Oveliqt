People find your business online. Then they message your competitor.

Most of the time it isn't a traffic problem. It's what happens in the first few seconds after someone lands on your site.

Here are four common leaks on service-business websites:

1. The first screen says who you are, not what you do for them. "Welcome to [Company]" tells a visitor nothing. "Custom office furniture, made in Kigali" tells them they're in the right place.

2. Enquiring takes too many steps. A nine-field contact form feels like paperwork. Most people would rather send a quick WhatsApp message.

3. Nothing shows you've done this before. No real photos, no named projects, no starting prices.

4. The enquiry arrives empty. "Hi, I'm interested" starts a back-and-forth before you even know what they want.

When we built ECOBE's website, we turned their printed catalogue into a site you can browse. Every piece has a one-tap WhatsApp or email button, and the message already names the item. The customer doesn't have to explain, and the team knows exactly what's being asked about.

Try this on your own site: open it on your phone and time how long it takes to send an enquiry. If it's more than 30 seconds, that's where your leads are going.

# Website Getting Visitors but No Enquiries? 7 Fixes for Service Businesses

You paid for a website. People are finding it. But the enquiries aren't coming in, and the few that do say "Hi, how much?" with no other detail.

This is a common problem for service businesses, and the good news is that it's rarely a traffic problem. It's a conversion problem: the website gets visitors and then fails to turn them into conversations.

Below are seven fixes, roughly in order from cheapest to most involved. You can check most of them yourself in an afternoon.

## First: confirm it really is a conversion problem

Before changing anything, check two things.

- **Is the traffic the right traffic?** If most visitors come from outside the area you serve, or land on a blog post that has nothing to do with what you sell, that's a visibility problem, not a website problem.
- **Which pages do people actually see?** If your About page gets more visits than your services pages, people may not be finding what they came for.

If the right people are reaching your service pages and still not contacting you, keep reading.

## 1. Say what you do on the first screen

Most visitors decide within seconds whether they're in the right place. The first screen (what you see before scrolling) needs to answer three questions:

- What do you do?
- Who is it for?
- Where do you work?

"Welcome to [Company], your trusted partner" answers none of them. "Custom office furniture, designed and made in Kigali" answers all three.

## 2. Make enquiring take one tap

Long contact forms feel like paperwork. Every extra field is another reason to leave.

In East Africa, many customers would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form. So put a WhatsApp button where people can see it, on every page and especially on mobile. Keep a short form (name, contact, message) for people who prefer email.

## 3. Pre-fill the enquiry

This small change makes a big difference to how fast you can reply. When someone taps "Enquire" on a product or service, the message can already say what they're asking about.

We did this for ECOBE, a steel, wood and interiors company in Kigali. They used to have a printed catalogue. Now it's a website you can browse, and every piece has a one-tap WhatsApp or email button whose message already names the item. The customer doesn't have to describe what they saw, and the team knows exactly what to reply about.

## 4. Show real work

Visitors are quietly asking, "Have they done this before?" Answer it with:

- photos of real projects (not stock images)
- named clients, where you have permission
- a short description of the problem you solved for each

One real project with a clear story is worth more than ten generic claims.

## 5. Give an idea of price

You don't have to publish a full price list. But "projects typically start from…" or "packages from…" helps people decide that you're in their range. Without any signal, many visitors assume you're too expensive and leave without asking.

## 6. Fix the mobile experience

Most of your visitors are probably on a phone. Open your site on yours and check:

- Does the first screen load quickly on mobile data?
- Can you read the text without zooming?
- Can you reach the contact button with your thumb?

If any answer is no, fix it before anything else on this list.

## 7. Reply fast, and track where leads come from

A website can do everything right and still lose the lead if nobody replies for two days. Decide who answers enquiries and how quickly.

Then start tracking. WhatsApp messages don't tell you where the person found you, so ask every new enquiry: "Where did you hear about us?" Write the answer down. After a month you'll know which pages and channels actually bring you work.

## A two-minute test you can do today

1. Open your website on your phone.
2. Ask: can I tell what this business does, for whom and where, without scrolling?
3. Time how long it takes to send an enquiry. Over 30 seconds means you're losing people.

## When it's time for a new website, and when it isn't

Many of these fixes are small edits: clearer words, a WhatsApp button, a few real photos. You may not need a new website at all.

A rebuild makes sense when the site is very slow, can't be edited, doesn't work on mobile, or no longer reflects what you sell.
