## Top trends

### 1. AI adoption among African SMBs is broad but shallow
- **What:** 2026 surveys show many small businesses using AI, but mostly for marketing and content rather than operations. In South Africa, 59% of active online sellers use AI for marketing/social media, while 35% use no AI at all; 54% cite cost as a barrier ⚠ ([iAfrica / Amazon report](https://iafrica.com/south-african-online-sellers-use-ai-for-marketing-not-operations-amazon-commissioned-survey/)). 52% use AI tools daily or weekly, and 56% want help understanding how AI applies to *their* operations ⚠ ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/state-of-south-african-small-business-2026/)). See also: [ITWeb, "SMMEs struggle to move beyond basic AI adoption"](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/smmes-struggle-to-move-beyond-basic-ai-adoption/raYAyqormreMJ38N), [Pan African Visions, Nov 2026](https://panafricanvisions.com/2026/11/artificial-intelligence-in-africa-majority-of-businesses-adopt-ai-technology-report-shows/).
- **Why the buyer cares:** owners already pay for AI tools without seeing operational gains. That fits Oveliq's honest-AI position ("only where it clearly beats a simpler solution") and its "one practical pilot" offer.
- **Caveat:** the data is mostly from South Africa, not Rwanda. Present it as "across Africa / in South Africa", not as Rwandan data.

### 2. Rwanda is formalising AI governance
- **What:** Rwanda has set up a national AI agency covering government transformation, skills, ethics and industry development ⚠ ([AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/rwanda-establishes-national-ai-agency-appoints-leaders)) and launched a National AI and Data Protection Strategy ⚠ ([af.net](https://af.net/realtime/rwanda-launches-national-ai-and-data-protection-strategy-to-advance-digital-sovereignty/)). The SME-relevant parts of the national AI policy are mostly procurement, challenges and an R&D risk-sharing fund ([AVODA Group](https://avodagroup.org/national-ai-strategies-east-africa-small-firms/)). A UNDP brief from April 2026 assesses Rwanda's AI potential ([UNDP PDF](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-04/policy_brief_assessment_of_rwandas_potential_in_ai_and_innovation.pdf)).
- **Why the buyer cares:** local organisations will hear "AI" from government and partners, and will want to know what is practical for them.
- **Caveat:** confirm dates and names on an official MINICT or government source before posting.

### 3. Data protection in Rwanda: "compliance is beyond registration"
- **What:** the NCSA's 2026 message is that holding a controller/processor certificate is only the start, and accountability is now the main obligation ([New Times](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/32861/opinions/why-2026-is-the-year-of-accountability-for-data-controllers-and-processors)). Storing or transferring personal data outside Rwanda needs NCSA authorisation and a written contract ([PwC Rwanda PDF](https://www.pwc.com/rw/en/assets/pdf/data-protection-in-rwand.pdf); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=RW&t=law)).
- **Why the buyer cares:** any website form, CRM or platform holding customer data (often on foreign cloud hosting) is in scope. This is a direct concern for "we've outgrown our spreadsheets" buyers.
- **Caveat:** Oveliq is not a law firm. Posts should frame this as "questions to ask", not legal advice.

### 4. The "traffic but no enquiries" problem
- **What:** agency content agrees widely that small-business sites fail on conversion, not traffic: unclear messaging, brochure-style pages, weak trust signals and complicated contact routes ([Big Red Jelly](https://bigredjelly.com/blog/website-traffic-conversion/); [Kind Fame](https://kindfame.co.uk/my-website-gets-no-enquiries/); [iFactory](https://ifactory.com.au/insights/getting-traffic-but-no-enquiries-heres-whats-really-going-wrong/)).
- **Why the buyer cares:** this matches buyer problem #1 on Oveliq's site ("People find us, but they don't contact us"), and ECOBE's one-tap WhatsApp enquiry is real proof against it.

### 5. WhatsApp automation is maturing for African SMBs
- **What:** 2026 guides draw a line between the free WhatsApp Business app (manual, small volume) and the paid API (automation, shared inboxes), with the switch suggested at roughly 50–100 chats/day ⚠ ([AfroTools](https://afrotools.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-africa-2026/); [Arkesel](https://arkesel.com/whatsapp-ai-features-business-south-africa/)). API access goes through Meta Business Solution Providers ([Ranking Kenya](https://rankingkenya.co.ke/whatsapp-business-api-africa)).
- **Why the buyer cares:** WhatsApp is already where East African customers enquire, and Oveliq's own primary contact route. Automation and integration is a service Oveliq sells.
- **Caveat:** all sources are vendor guides, and I found no independent news coverage.
